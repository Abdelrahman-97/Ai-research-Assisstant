Title: Effect of a new antihypertensive intervention on systolic blood pressure

Background and aim:
Hypertension is a major cardiovascular risk factor. This study evaluated whether a
new antihypertensive intervention lowers systolic blood pressure compared with usual
care.

Design:
A two-arm parallel randomised study. Participants were allocated to either the
treatment group (new intervention) or the control group (usual care). The two groups
are independent (each participant is in only one group).

Participants:
Adult patients with stage-1 hypertension. Both groups are comparable in age and sex.

Outcome:
The primary outcome is systolic blood pressure (mmHg), a continuous measurement, taken
after the intervention period. The variable in the dataset is "systolic_bp", and the
group allocation is the variable "group" (control vs treatment). "age" and "sex" are
recorded as baseline characteristics.

Hypothesis:
The treatment group has a lower mean systolic blood pressure than the control group.
We compare the mean systolic blood pressure between the two independent groups.
